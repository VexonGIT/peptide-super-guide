The foundation

What peptides are, and why the category gets messy

Peptides are chains of amino acids. Some occur naturally in the body, some are engineered into prescription medicines, and some are marketed online with claims that outpace the evidence.

Biology

Signal molecules

Many peptides help cells communicate. They can act like hormones, messengers, receptor ligands, or local signaling molecules.

Medicine

Real drugs exist

FDA-approved peptide drug products are developed for specific indications and evaluated through manufacturing, pharmacology, safety, and efficacy requirements.

Market

Claims vary widely

Online peptide claims often blend approved medicines, compounded products, cosmetics, supplements, and research chemicals into one confusing bucket.

Category map

Four lanes to keep separate

Lane
What it means
Practical stance
Approved peptide medicine
A regulated drug product with a labeled indication, manufacturer, and prescribing information.
Discuss risks, benefits, and monitoring with a licensed clinician.
Compounded medication
A pharmacy-prepared medication for a patient-specific need, with rules and limits that can change.
Verify the prescriber, pharmacy, formula, labeling, and current FDA status.
Cosmetic or supplement claim
Often sold for skin, hair, body composition, recovery, or performance claims.
Treat health claims cautiously and look for human clinical evidence.
Research-only peptide
A compound labeled for laboratory research rather than patient treatment.
Do not treat research-only labeling as a workaround for medical use.

Regulated medicine lane

Peptide or peptide-like medicines people may recognize

Semaglutide, liraglutide, tirzepatide

Incretin-based medicines used under specific approved labels for diabetes and/or chronic weight management.

Key check: approved product, indication, and source.

Glucagon

A peptide hormone medicine used in specific hypoglycemia and diagnostic contexts.

Key check: emergency plan and product instructions.

Teriparatide, abaloparatide

Parathyroid hormone analogs used for selected osteoporosis patients under clinician supervision.

Key check: duration, bone-risk context, monitoring.

Desmopressin

A vasopressin analog used for specific bleeding, endocrine, and fluid-balance indications.

Key check: sodium and fluid-risk monitoring.

Octreotide, lanreotide

Somatostatin analogs used in specialist care for conditions such as acromegaly and certain neuroendocrine tumors.

Key check: specialist diagnosis and follow-up.

Leuprolide, goserelin

GnRH analogs used in hormone-sensitive conditions under diagnosis-specific protocols.

Key check: indication, fertility, and bone-health context.

Tesamorelin

A growth hormone-releasing factor analog approved for a narrow HIV-associated lipodystrophy indication.

Key check: narrow label, metabolic monitoring.

Bivalirudin, eptifibatide

Peptide or peptide-derived antithrombotic medicines used in tightly controlled cardiovascular settings.

Key check: hospital-level bleeding-risk controls.
Topical and cosmetic lane

Cosmetic peptides are a different evidence bucket

GHK-Cu / copper peptides

Usually discussed in skin-care contexts. Topical cosmetic evidence should not be treated like drug evidence.

Palmitoyl pentapeptide-4

A common cosmetic signal-peptide ingredient category used in anti-aging marketing.

Acetyl hexapeptide-8

A topical cosmetic peptide often marketed around expression-line appearance claims.

Palmitoyl tripeptides

A family of skin-care peptides where formula quality and human evidence matter more than ingredient buzz.

High-caution online lane

Research and wellness peptides people search for

BPC-157

FDA has flagged compounded BPC-157 for safety uncertainty, immunogenicity concerns, and peptide impurity complexity.

CJC-1295

FDA has identified serious adverse events and limited available clinical data for compounded CJC-1295.

MOTs-C

FDA states it lacks important human safety information for compounded MOTs-C.

Melanotan II

FDA cites case reports of serious adverse events and safety concerns for compounded Melanotan II.

Epitalon, Semax, Selank, DSIP

FDA lists multiple compounds in this area as having inadequate safety information for proposed compounded use.

Thymosin alpha-1 and related claims

FDA flags inadequate safety understanding for compounded thymosin-alpha 1; athletic rules may also matter.

Clinician questions

Questions worth asking directly

Is this peptide approved for my condition?

Ask for the exact product name, indication, expected benefit, common risks, serious risks, and the reason it fits your health history.

What monitoring is needed?

Monitoring can include symptoms, labs, medication interactions, pregnancy considerations, allergies, and follow-up timing.

Who made it and who dispensed it?

Legitimate products should have clear manufacturer or pharmacy information, labeling, storage instructions, and a way to report adverse events.

What would make me stop?

Ask for concrete warning signs and contact instructions before starting any prescribed medication.

Glossary

Terms that prevent mix-ups

Peptide
A short chain of amino acids connected by peptide bonds.
Analog
A molecule modified to behave like, or improve on, a natural peptide.
Indication
The specific condition or use a medicine is approved or studied for.
Immunogenicity
The potential for a product to trigger an immune response.
Compounding
Pharmacy preparation of a medication for a patient-specific need under applicable rules.
Research-only
A label indicating a compound is not being sold as a patient medicine.
