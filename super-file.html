The Short Version

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids. Some peptides are real prescription medicines. Some are skin-care ingredients. Some are research drugs. Some are sold online with claims that run ahead of the proof.

GreenFDA-approved medicine for a real diagnosis.
YellowHuman research exists, but the use is not approved.
RedResearch-only, gray-market, or FDA safety concern.

Evidence Key

A strong body mechanism does not prove a real health benefit. A compound can look good in cells or animals and still fail in people.

StrongFDA-approved use or several good human trials.
ModerateMultiple human studies, but limits remain.
LimitedSmall trials, regional studies, or mixed results.
Very lowMostly animal, cell, or anecdotal data.

Ask These Before Believing A Claim

  • Was this exact compound tested?
  • Was it tested in people?
  • Was there a placebo group?
  • Was the study large enough and long enough?
  • Did it measure a real health result, not just a lab marker?
  • Were side effects reported?
  • Did another research group repeat the result?

Big 2026 Updates

  • FDA says compounded drugs are not FDA-approved and are not checked by FDA for safety, effect, or quality before sale.
  • FDA says retatrutide and cagrilintide cannot be used in compounding under federal law.
  • FDA says semaglutide salt forms are different active ingredients than approved semaglutide drugs.
  • FDA warns that some online GLP-1 products are sold falsely as "research only" while being marketed for human use.
  • FDA lists many popular online peptides as bulk substances that may present significant safety risks.
  • Athletes need a separate WADA and sport-rule check before using any peptide-related product.

Weight-Loss And Metabolic Drugs

Semaglutide

Status: FDA-approved products exist.

Job: Copies a GLP-1 signal. It can lower appetite, slow stomach emptying, and help blood sugar.

Careful: Approved products are not the same as every compounded or online product.

Tirzepatide

Status: FDA-approved products exist.

Job: Works on GIP and GLP-1 pathways.

Careful: Online "research" tirzepatide is not the same as an FDA-approved product.

Retatrutide

Status: Investigational.

Job: Designed to act on GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon pathways.

Careful: FDA says it cannot be used in compounding under federal law.

Cagrilintide

Status: Investigational.

Job: An amylin analog being studied for appetite and weight.

Careful: Research interest is not patient access.

Growth Hormone Axis

This lane can affect growth hormone and IGF-1 signaling. It can also affect blood sugar, swelling, joint symptoms, sleep apnea, and cancer-screening concerns. A higher lab number does not prove a better health result.

Tesamorelin

Status: FDA-approved for a narrow use.

Bottom line: For HIV-associated lipodystrophy. Not a general weight-loss drug.

CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6

Status: Not approved as general wellness drugs.

Bottom line: Treat GH-axis stacks as high-medical-complexity, not routine wellness tools.

Healing And Recovery

This is the category with the most online hype.

BPC-157

Status: Unapproved.

Claim: Tendon, gut, nerve, or injury recovery.

Bottom line: No proven human tendon, gut, or nerve protocol.

TB-500

Status: Unapproved.

Claim: Recovery and tissue repair.

Bottom line: Marketed fragments are not the same as full thymosin beta-4.

KPV And LL-37

Status: Unapproved.

Claim: Immune, gut, or inflammation support.

Bottom line: Immune-related claims need extra caution.

Skin, Pigment, And Cosmetic Peptides

Topical GHK-Cu And Cosmetic Peptides

Status: Cosmetic lane.

Topical skin-care use is not the same as injectable use. FDA flags injectable GHK-Cu for safety gaps.

Melanotan II

Status: Unapproved.

FDA cites serious case reports for compounded Melanotan II, including melanoma and other serious events.

Afamelanotide

Status: FDA-approved narrow use.

For adults with erythropoietic protoporphyria, a rare light-sensitivity condition. Not a tanning product.

Longevity, Brain, And Sleep

MOTs-C

Status: Unapproved.

FDA says human exposure data for drug products containing MOTs-C are lacking.

Epitalon

Status: Unapproved.

No proven anti-aging or lifespan result for general use.

Dihexa

Status: Research-only.

FDA says it has not identified human exposure data for drug products containing dihexa acetate.

Semax, Selank, DSIP

Status: Not FDA-approved.

Small or regional evidence is not U.S. drug approval.

Sexual And Reproductive Health

Bremelanotide / PT-141

Status: FDA-approved narrow use.

Approved for acquired, generalized HSDD in certain premenopausal women. Not approved for men or general performance.

Kisspeptin-10 And Kisspeptin-54

Status: Research area.

Fertility research does not equal a general hormone protocol.

Oxytocin

Status: Prescription medicine for narrow uses.

Social bonding, mood, or relationship claims are not the same as approved medical use.

Stacks, Blends, And Protocols

A stack means using more than one compound. This can make the story confusing.

Why stacks are risky

  • You may not know which compound helped.
  • You may not know which compound caused a side effect.
  • Side effects can add up.
  • Drug interactions can be missed.

Monitoring Questions

There is no one lab panel that makes every peptide safe. Monitoring depends on the exact product, diagnosis, medical history, and other medicines.

  • What diagnosis is this for?
  • Is this product FDA-approved for that use?
  • What benefits are realistic?
  • What side effects are common?
  • What serious risks should I know?
  • What medical history makes this a bad fit?
  • What medicines or supplements can interact with it?
  • What labs or follow-up are needed?
  • What symptoms mean I should stop and call?
  • How do I report a side effect?

Red Flags And When To Get Help

Stop trusting the seller if

  • The product says "research only" but includes human-use instructions.
  • The seller says no doctor is needed.
  • The label is missing key product details.
  • The seller says compounded means FDA-approved.
  • The product arrives warm, damaged, cloudy, leaking, or unlabeled.

Get urgent medical help for

  • Trouble breathing or swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat.
  • Chest pain, fainting, confusion, or sudden vision changes.
  • Severe or lasting belly pain.
  • Severe vomiting or signs of dehydration.
  • Fever, spreading redness, pus, or severe pain at an injection site.

Glossary

Peptide: A short chain of amino acids.

Analog: A changed version of a natural molecule.

FDA-approved: FDA reviewed a specific drug for a specific use.

Compounded drug: A pharmacy-prepared drug for a patient-specific need. Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved.

Research-only: A label that means the product is not being sold as a medicine.

COA: A certificate of analysis. It does not prove sterility, stability, safety, or benefit.
