The Short Version

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids. Some peptides are real prescription medicines. Some are skin-care ingredients. Some are research chemicals sold online with big claims.

GreenFDA-approved medicine for a real diagnosis.
YellowHuman research exists, but the use is not approved.
RedResearch-only, gray-market, or FDA safety concern.

Big 2026 Updates

  • FDA says compounded GLP-1 drugs are not FDA-approved and can have quality, dosing, label, and shipping problems.
  • FDA says retatrutide and cagrilintide cannot be used in compounding under federal law.
  • FDA says semaglutide salt forms, like semaglutide sodium and semaglutide acetate, are different active ingredients than approved semaglutide drugs.
  • FDA warns that some online GLP-1 products are sold falsely as "research only" while being marketed for human use.
  • FDA lists many popular online peptides as bulk substances that may present significant safety risks.

Green Lane Examples

These are examples of peptide or peptide-like medicines with FDA-approved uses. They still need a clinician and a real medical reason.

Semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutideDiabetes and/or chronic weight management, depending on the product label.
GlucagonEmergency low blood sugar and some diagnostic uses.
DesmopressinSelected fluid-balance, bleeding, or endocrine uses. Low sodium is a key risk.
Octreotide, lanreotideSpecialist uses such as acromegaly and some neuroendocrine tumors.
Teriparatide, abaloparatideSelected osteoporosis patients.
TesamorelinA narrow HIV-associated lipodystrophy use. Not general weight loss.
Bremelanotide / PT-141A narrow HSDD use in certain premenopausal women. Not a general performance drug.
AfamelanotideA narrow erythropoietic protoporphyria light-exposure use.
